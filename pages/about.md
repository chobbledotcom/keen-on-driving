      ## Learning to teach, properly

      While he was training, Daniel was mentored by Andy Scanlon of Scan School of Motoring. Andy came to instructing after a career in swimming coaching and had followed his dad into the job, so he had seen teaching from more than one angle. He guided Daniel through the parts of the job that the test does not cover: how to explain something a second way when the first one has not landed, how to keep a nervous pupil moving forward, and how to judge when somebody is genuinely ready for their test.

      That matters because the qualification is not easy to get. Only around 35% of people who train to become instructors pass the final Part 3 test nationally. Daniel was one of them, and he now uses what he learned from both Linda and Andy with every pupil.

      Daniel understands that pupils learn differently.


      More confident drivers can be more likely to take risks, while less

      ## What that looks like in the car

      Lessons are two hours, one-to-one, in a manual Citroën C3. Two hours is long enough to warm up, work on something specific and then use it in traffic, which is why Daniel does not offer one-hour lessons.

      There is no fixed script. A pupil who is struggling with clutch control might spend a lesson on quiet residential streets until it settles. A pupil who is nearly test ready might spend the whole two hours on the routes around Nelson, with the test centre's busier junctions and roundabouts in the mix. The lesson follows the pupil, not a syllabus on a wall.

      If you want to know what that looks like for you, the [prices page](/prices/) explains the lesson options, and the [reviews page](/reviews/) has every review in full.

      ## A note from Daniel

      I set up Keen On Driving because I wanted to teach one person at a time, at their pace. That is easier said than done in a car, but two hours helps: it gives us time to get somewhere quiet, work on whatever is not clicking, and then drive back through traffic while it is still fresh.

      I do not promise a pass in a set number of lessons. I will tell you honestly when you are ready, and I will tell you what still needs work if you are not. If you want to know what that looks like for you, get in touch.
